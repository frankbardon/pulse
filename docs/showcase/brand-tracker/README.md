# Brand tracker: weighting, banner tables and significance testing

A consumer brand runs a quarterly brand-health survey. Four waves were fielded in 2025, with about 1,500 US adults per wave. The insight team has two jobs each quarter: tell leadership whether the brand moved, and hand the client a weighted banner table plus an SPSS file. This example does both with Pulse, from generating the data to producing the `.sav` file.

The data is synthetic and was generated by Pulse from a spec with a fixed seed. Because we built it, we know which movements are real and which are noise. That lets us check each analytical choice against the truth.

## The business question

1. **Did the brand move in 2025?** We track awareness (unaided and aided), consideration, purchase intent (top-2-box) and Net Promoter Score (NPS), and say which wave-over-wave changes are real.
2. **Who is the brand strong with right now?** This is the Q4 banner: KPIs and brand-image attributes by gender, age and region, with significance letters.
3. **What can we tell the client, and what can we not?** Some of the answers change depending on whether the data is weighted and whether the tests are corrected for multiple comparisons.

## Headline findings

| # | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The unaided-awareness "gain" is a sampling artefact.** Unweighted, unaided awareness rises from 32.3% in Q1 to 38.3% in Q4 (p < 0.001). Weighted to census, it goes from 29.1% to 28.8% (p = 0.867). Q4 fieldwork over-delivered 18–34s: they were 59.3% of the Q4 sample against a census share of 29%. | Steps 1–3 |
| 2 | **Weighting flips the sign of NPS.** Unweighted NPS is +5.6 (95% CI +3.3 to +8.0). Weighted NPS is −3.2 (95% CI −5.8 to −0.6). Neither interval contains zero, so the two answers really do disagree. The cause is the same young skew: 18–34s score +28, while people aged 55 and over score −29. | Step 4 |
| 3 | **Consideration really did grow.** Among aware adults, weighted consideration rose from 34.3% in Q1 to 51.8% in Q4. The Q3→Q4 step of +8.3 points survives a Holm correction across all 30 tracker tests (adjusted p = 0.012). The Q2→Q3 step of +6.1 points is significant uncorrected (p = 0.004) but not after correction (0.115). | Step 5 |
| 4 | **Multiple-comparison correction removes the false alarms.** Uncorrected, the tracker reports an aided-awareness dip in Q4 (−3.6 points, p = 0.046). The Q4 banner reports a West-over-Midwest lead on top-2-box intent (37.6% vs 27.9%, p = 0.046). Both are noise in the generating model, and neither survives Holm (adjusted p = 1.000 for each). | Steps 5–6 |
| 5 | **"Eco-friendly" is the image story.** Association with "Eco-friendly" doubled, from 12.2% in Q1 to 23.6% in Q4 among aware adults. Every quarter, people who make that association are more likely to consider the brand: 58.9% vs 49.6% in Q4. | Steps 9–10 |

The practical implication: report the brand as **stable on awareness and NPS (slightly negative), and growing on consideration, with the eco message as the most likely driver.** The unweighted deck would have reported an awareness win and a positive NPS. Both are wrong.

## Dataset

`spec/brand_tracker.synth.json` holds the generating spec. `pulse synth from-schema --seed 2025` turns it into `brand_tracker.pulse`: 6,000 respondents and 13 fields.

| Field | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `resp_id` | `u32` | Respondent ID, 100001… |
| `market` | `categorical_u8` | Always `US`; used as the "Total" banner column |
| `wave` | `categorical_u8` | `W1`…`W4` (label table `wave_names` → "2025 Q1"…"2025 Q4") |
| `age_band` | `categorical_u8` | `18-34`, `35-54`, `55+` |
| `gender` | `categorical_u8` | `Female`, `Male` |
| `region` | `categorical_u8` | `NE`, `MW`, `SO`, `WE` (label table `region_names`) |
| `weight` | `f64` | Post-stratification weight to census age × gender × region |
| `aware_unaided` | `packed_bool` | Named the brand unprompted |
| `aware_aided` | `packed_bool` | Recognised the brand from a list |
| `consider` | `packed_bool`, nullable | Would consider; asked only if aided-aware |
| `intent` | `u8`, nullable | Purchase intent 1–5; asked only if aided-aware |
| `nps` | `u8`, nullable | Likelihood to recommend 0–10; asked only if aided-aware |
| `attributes` | `set_u8`, nullable | Multi-select brand image: Good value, High quality, Innovative, Trustworthy, Stylish, Eco-friendly, For people like me, None of these; asked only if aided-aware |

### Structure and ground truth built into the spec

**The sample is skewed young, and Q4 is worse.** A `categorical_pairs` entry draws `age_band` conditional on `wave`. In Q1–Q3 the draw is 40% / 36% / 24% (18–34 / 35–54 / 55+). In Q4 it is 58% / 28% / 14%. Census targets are 29% / 33% / 38%. Women (55% of the sample vs 51% census) and the Northeast are also over-represented.

**Weights are census share divided by design share.** The weight is computed per cell (wave × age, then gender and region) by a synth `rules[].set_expr`. Pulse checks the result in Step 1.

**Each KPI has a planted signal and noise.** Each KPI is a synth `models` entry: an ordered-probit shift by age band and, for consideration only, by wave and by the Eco-friendly image bit.

| KPI | What drives it |
|---|---|
| Unaided and aided awareness | Strongly age-driven; **flat across waves** |
| Consideration | Age, plus a **real lift in Q3 and a larger one in Q4** (a campaign), plus a boost for respondents who see the brand as Eco-friendly |
| Purchase intent | Mildly age-driven; flat across waves and regions |
| NPS | Strongly age-driven (young promoters, older detractors); flat across waves and regions |

**Brand image (multi-select).** `set_bernoulli` draws each option, and `set_categorical_pairs` makes some options depend on other fields:

| Option | Depends on |
|---|---|
| Innovative, Stylish, For people like me | Age (young higher) |
| Eco-friendly | Wave: 12% → 13% → 19% → 24% |
| Trustworthy | Region: Midwest 42% vs 35% elsewhere |

**Question routing.** Structural rules make unaided awareness imply aided awareness. They also null consideration, intent, NPS and image for the not-aware, and set "None of these" when nothing else was ticked.

## Method

- **Weights.** Every figure marked "weighted" uses `{"weight": {"field": "weight", "kind": "probability"}}` on the request. Weighted inference uses the Kish effective sample size, so the tests account for the variance the weights add. Where a step compares weighted and unweighted figures side by side in one request, the unweighted slots opt out with `"weight": null`.
- **Bases.** Awareness uses all adults. Consideration, top-2-box intent, NPS and image use the aided-aware: those questions were only asked of them. Top-2-box is `ATTR_CODE_IN` on intent codes 4 and 5. NPS is the mean of a score that is +100 for 9–10, −100 for 0–6 and 0 otherwise, so the mean *is* the NPS.
- **Significance.** Proportions use `OVERLAY_PAIRWISE_PROP_Z` on a weighted mean-of-indicator cell. The NPS mean uses `OVERLAY_PAIRWISE_WEIGHTED_TWO_MEANS_Z` with `n_basis: "kish"`. All tests are two-sided at α = 0.05. Corrections use a `multiplicity` block on the Compose request with `family: "compose"`, which pools every test in the request into one family.

Every step below is one file in `req/`. Run it with `pulse api process` (a single request) or `pulse api compose` (a `requests` batch), after `pulse api predict` / `predict-compose`. `PULSE_LABEL_TABLES_DIR=./labels` registers the two label tables. `run.sh` runs all of it.

## Walkthrough

### Step 0: Generate the cohort

```sh
pulse synth from-schema -s spec/brand_tracker.synth.json -o brand_tracker.pulse --seed 2025 --json
pulse cohort inspect brand_tracker.pulse --json
```

The run returned `rows_generated: 6000` and `rows_rejected: 0`, with no warnings. The same seed produces a byte-identical file.

### Step 1: How far is the sample from census, and what does weighting cost? (`req/01_sample_vs_census.json`)

This is a Compose batch of nine slots. Eight are crosstabs of `AGG_COUNT` with `"normalize": "column"`, run once without and once with the weight. The ninth is a weight profile by wave: min and max weight with the weight switched off, and a weighted count whose components carry the Kish `n_eff`.

```json
{"label": "age_by_wave_weighted", "cohort": {"filename": "brand_tracker.pulse", "data_dir": "."},
 "weight": {"field": "weight", "kind": "probability"},
 "crosstab": {"rows": [{"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "age_band"}],
              "columns": [{"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "wave"}],
              "cell": {"type": "AGG_COUNT", "field": "resp_id", "label": "share"},
              "normalize": "column"}}
```

**Age share by wave, unweighted → weighted** (census target in brackets):

| Age | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | All waves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18–34 (29%) | 42.1% → 30.7% | 41.5% → 30.4% | 40.2% → 28.6% | **59.3% → 30.1%** | 45.7% → 30.0% |
| 35–54 (33%) | 34.3% → 31.9% | 34.9% → 32.1% | 34.3% → 31.3% | 27.5% → 33.1% | 32.8% → 32.1% |
| 55+ (38%) | 23.6% → 37.3% | 23.6% → 37.5% | 25.5% → 40.1% | 13.2% → 36.8% | 21.5% → 38.0% |

Gender goes from 55.6% / 44.4% (female / male) to 51.3% / 48.7%. Region (NE / MW / SO / WE) goes from 19.1% / 22.8% / 36.0% / 22.2% to 16.0% / 22.1% / 38.0% / 24.0%. Across all waves, every weighted share lands within 1.0 point of its target, and each single wave lands within 2.1 points. These are design weights, not raked weights: the residual gap is sampling noise in each wave's realised mix.

**The price of the Q4 skew is precision:**

| Wave | Respondents | Min weight | Max weight | Weighted base | Kish n_eff | Efficiency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | 1,541 | 0.57 | 1.88 | 1,522 | 1,366 | 89% |
| Q2 | 1,469 | 0.57 | 1.88 | 1,465 | 1,300 | 89% |
| Q3 | 1,509 | 0.57 | 1.88 | 1,526 | 1,328 | 88% |
| Q4 | 1,481 | 0.39 | 3.22 | 1,456 | **926** | **63%** |

Q4's 1,481 interviews are worth 926 unweighted interviews. Every Q4 test below has less power than its headcount suggests.

### Step 2: The funnel by wave, weighted vs unweighted, in one request (`req/02_funnel_by_wave.json`)

One grouped Process. The request-level weight applies to every slot. The `_unweighted` slots override it with `"weight": null`, and `pulse api predict` lists each slot's resolution (`applied` / `opted_out`) before anything runs. A label binding turns wave codes into quarter names.

```json
{"cohort": {"filename": "brand_tracker.pulse", "data_dir": "."},
 "weight": {"field": "weight", "kind": "probability"},
 "groups": [{"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "wave"}],
 "aggregations": [
   {"type": "AGG_COUNT", "field": "resp_id", "label": "respondents", "weight": null},
   {"type": "AGG_AVERAGE", "field": "aware_unaided", "label": "unaided_weighted"},
   {"type": "AGG_AVERAGE", "field": "aware_unaided", "label": "unaided_unweighted", "weight": null},
   "... same pair for aware_aided and consider ..."],
 "labels": [{"field": "wave", "table": "wave_names", "mode": "replace"}]}
```

Each cell shows unweighted / weighted.

| Wave | Respondents | Aided awareness | Unaided awareness | Consideration (aware base) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 Q1 | 1,541 | 80.4% / 78.6% | 32.3% / 29.1% | 35.0% / 34.3% |
| 2025 Q2 | 1,469 | 79.4% / 76.8% | 36.1% / 31.8% | 38.3% / 37.4% |
| 2025 Q3 | 1,509 | 80.5% / 78.3% | 35.9% / 32.0% | 43.4% / 43.5% |
| 2025 Q4 | 1,481 | 81.0% / 74.7% | **38.3% / 28.8%** | 52.2% / 51.8% |

Weighting moves awareness by 1.8–9.5 points because awareness is age-driven. It moves consideration by less than 1 point, because consideration depends little on age.

### Step 3: Is the unaided gain real? (`req/03_unaided_phantom_gain.json`)

Two Compose slots, identical except for the weight. Each is a crosstab with waves as rows and the single-value `market` field as the column, so there is one "Total" column. `AGG_AVERAGE` of the 0/1 field gives the proportion. `OVERLAY_PAIRWISE_PROP_Z` at `row` scope then tests every pair of waves. On the weighted slot the test reads each cell's Kish `n_eff`.

```json
{"label": "weighted", "cohort": {"filename": "brand_tracker.pulse", "data_dir": "."},
 "weight": {"field": "weight", "kind": "probability"},
 "crosstab": {"rows": [{"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "wave"}],
              "columns": [{"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "market"}],
              "cell": {"type": "AGG_AVERAGE", "field": "aware_unaided", "label": "unaided"}},
 "overlays": [{"name": "wave_vs_wave", "kind": "OVERLAY_PAIRWISE_PROP_Z", "scope": "row",
               "params": {"p_source": "cell_value"}}]}
```

| Wave pair | Unweighted p | Weighted p |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 vs Q2 | 0.030 | 0.136 |
| Q1 vs Q3 | 0.039 | 0.101 |
| **Q1 vs Q4** | **< 0.001** (32.3% → 38.3%) | **0.867** (29.1% → 28.8%) |
| Q2 vs Q3 | 0.897 | 0.891 |
| Q2 vs Q4 | 0.215 | 0.132 |
| Q3 vs Q4 | 0.168 | 0.101 |

Unweighted, there are three "significant" gains on unaided awareness, including a 6-point year-on-year rise. Weighted, there are none. The spec has no wave effect on awareness. The unweighted gain comes entirely from interviewing more 18–34s, who are 48% unaided-aware against 13% for people aged 55 and over (Step 6).

### Step 4: NPS changes sign under weighting (`req/04_nps_reversal.json`)

Two slots: total and by age band. Each computes the NPS mean and its 95% confidence bounds (`AGG_CI_LOWER` / `AGG_CI_UPPER`) both weighted and with `"weight": null`. The formula attribute turns the 0–10 answer into a −100/0/+100 score. A `FILTER_NULL` restricts the base to respondents who were asked the question.

```json
{"label": "total", "cohort": {"filename": "brand_tracker.pulse", "data_dir": "."},
 "weight": {"field": "weight", "kind": "probability"},
 "filterers": [{"type": "FILTER_NULL", "field": "nps", "values": ["is_not_null"]}],
 "attributes": [{"type": "ATTR_FORMULA", "field": "nps", "label": "nps_score",
                 "expression": "nps >= 9 ? 100 : (nps <= 6 ? -100 : 0)"}],
 "aggregations": [
   {"type": "AGG_AVERAGE", "field": "nps_score", "label": "nps_unweighted", "weight": null},
   {"type": "AGG_CI_LOWER", "field": "nps_score", "label": "nps_ci_low_unweighted", "weight": null},
   {"type": "AGG_AVERAGE", "field": "nps_score", "label": "nps_weighted"},
   {"type": "AGG_CI_LOWER", "field": "nps_score", "label": "nps_ci_low_weighted"},
   "... CI_UPPER pair, respondent count ..."]}
```

| Base | Respondents | NPS unweighted (95% CI) | NPS weighted (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **All aware** | 4,818 | **+5.6** (+3.3 to +8.0) | **−3.2** (−5.8 to −0.6) |
| 18–34 | 2,389 | +28.6 (+25.4 to +31.8) | +28.2 (+24.9 to +31.4) |
| 35–54 | 1,537 | −9.7 (−13.8 to −5.6) | −9.0 (−13.1 to −4.8) |
| 55+ | 892 | −29.6 (−34.6 to −24.6) | −29.2 (−34.4 to −23.9) |

Within each age band the weighted and unweighted NPS agree to within 0.7 points. The total differs only because the bands are mixed in different proportions. The unweighted mix is half 18–34s, because the young are both over-sampled and more often aware. That alone turns a slightly negative NPS into a positive one.

### Step 5: The KPI tracker: deltas and tests, corrected across all KPIs (`req/05_kpi_tracker.json`)

This is a ten-slot Compose with two slots per KPI:

- A grouped Process by wave with `OVERLAY_DELTA_VS_PRIOR`. It gives the change against the previous wave in the KPI's own units.
- The Step 3 crosstab with pairwise wave tests.

A request-level `"multiplicity": {"method": "holm", "family": "compose"}` pools all 30 pairwise p-values (5 KPIs × 6 wave pairs). Each overlay then carries `p_adjusted` beside the untouched raw `p_value`.

```json
{"requests": [
   {"label": "consider_delta", "...": "...",
    "groups": [{"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "wave"}],
    "aggregations": [{"type": "AGG_AVERAGE", "field": "consider", "label": "consider"}],
    "overlays": [{"name": "delta_vs_prior_wave", "kind": "OVERLAY_DELTA_VS_PRIOR", "scope": "group"}]},
   {"label": "consider_tests", "...": "wave x market crosstab as in Step 3",
    "overlays": [{"name": "wave_vs_wave", "kind": "OVERLAY_PAIRWISE_PROP_Z", "scope": "row",
                  "params": {"p_source": "cell_value"}}]},
   "... four more KPI pairs; NPS uses OVERLAY_PAIRWISE_WEIGHTED_TWO_MEANS_Z with n_basis kish ..."],
 "multiplicity": {"method": "holm", "family": "compose"}}
```

All figures are weighted. Each change column shows the delta, then the raw p, then the Holm p (m = 30).

| KPI | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Q1→Q2 | Q2→Q3 | Q3→Q4 | Q1→Q4 p / Holm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aided awareness | 78.6% | 76.8% | 78.3% | 74.7% | −1.8 pts · 0.266 · 1.000 | +1.5 pts · 0.370 · 1.000 | **−3.6 pts · 0.046 · 1.000** | 0.028 / 0.689 |
| Unaided awareness | 29.1% | 31.8% | 32.0% | 28.8% | +2.7 pts · 0.136 · 1.000 | +0.2 pts · 0.891 · 1.000 | −3.2 pts · 0.101 · 1.000 | 0.867 / 1.000 |
| Consideration | 34.3% | 37.4% | 43.5% | 51.8% | +3.1 pts · 0.141 · 1.000 | **+6.1 pts · 0.004 · 0.115** | **+8.3 pts · < 0.001 · 0.012** | < 0.001 / < 0.001 |
| Intent top-2-box | 35.3% | 33.8% | 32.1% | 32.5% | −1.5 pts · 0.462 · 1.000 | −1.7 pts · 0.396 · 1.000 | +0.5 pts · 0.833 · 1.000 | 0.214 / 1.000 |
| NPS | −5.0 | −2.7 | −5.6 | +1.0 | +2.4 · 0.514 · 1.000 | −2.9 · 0.421 · 1.000 | +6.5 · 0.097 · 1.000 | 0.128 / 1.000 |

Read against the ground truth:

- **The Q4 dip in aided awareness is a false positive.** It is significant uncorrected (p = 0.046) and the spec has no wave effect on awareness. Holm removes it (1.000).
- **The Q3→Q4 consideration lift is real and survives correction** (adjusted p = 0.012). So does the full-year gain (34.3% → 51.8%, p < 0.001).
- **The Q2→Q3 consideration step is also real.** The spec plants a Q3 lift, but Holm (0.115) cannot confirm it with these sample sizes. Correction protects against false alarms at the cost of power.

### Step 6: The Q4 banner with significance letters (`req/06_q4_banner.json`)

The banner is a 15-slot Compose: 5 KPIs × 3 banner variables (gender, age band, region). It uses Q4 interviews only and is weighted throughout.

- Each slot is a one-row crosstab (`market` × banner variable). `margins.rows` supplies the Total.
- Each slot carries a `column`-scope pairwise test, which compares every pair of banner columns.
- One `multiplicity` block pools all 50 tests.

Here is one slot exactly as sent:

```json
{"label": "nps__age_band", "cohort": {"filename": "brand_tracker.pulse", "data_dir": "."},
 "weight": {"field": "weight", "kind": "probability"},
 "filterers": [{"type": "FILTER_INCLUDE", "field": "wave", "values": ["W4"]},
               {"type": "FILTER_NULL", "field": "nps", "values": ["is_not_null"]}],
 "attributes": [{"type": "ATTR_FORMULA", "field": "nps", "label": "nps_score",
                 "expression": "nps >= 9 ? 100 : (nps <= 6 ? -100 : 0)"}],
 "crosstab": {"rows": [{"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "market"}],
              "columns": [{"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "age_band"}],
              "cell": {"type": "AGG_AVERAGE", "field": "nps_score", "label": "nps"},
              "margins": {"rows": true}},
 "overlays": [{"name": "column_tests", "kind": "OVERLAY_PAIRWISE_WEIGHTED_TWO_MEANS_Z", "scope": "column",
               "params": {"n_basis": "kish"}}]}
```

The other 14 slots differ only in the KPI field (top-2-box adds the `ATTR_CODE_IN` attribute) and the column field. The proportion KPIs use `OVERLAY_PAIRWISE_PROP_Z`. The request ends with `"multiplicity": {"method": "holm", "family": "compose"}`.

**Q4 2025 banner, weighted.** Pulse returns raw p-values, Holm-adjusted p-values and the cell values. A letter means the column is significantly higher than the lettered column within the same banner group (raw p < 0.05). ~~Struck~~ letters do not survive Holm across the 50 tests.

| KPI | Total | A Female | B Male | C 18–34 | D 35–54 | E 55+ | F NE | G MW | H SO | I WE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aided awareness | 74.7% | 76.0% | 73.4% | 86.5% DE | 77.4% E | 62.4% | 78.0% | 73.4% | 71.4% | 78.9% |
| Unaided awareness | 28.8% | 30.0% | 27.7% | 48.2% DE | 28.6% E | 13.0% | 33.2% | 27.3% | 25.4% | 32.8% |
| Consideration | 51.8% | 52.3% | 51.3% | 52.4% | 52.0% | 50.9% | 46.9% | 48.3% | 57.1% | 50.9% |
| Intent top-2-box | 32.5% | 33.6% | 31.5% | 34.2% | 34.1% | 28.9% | 29.1% | 27.9% | 33.8% | 37.6% ~~G~~ |
| NPS | +1.0 | +0.5 | +1.4 | +30.7 DE | −9.1 | −21.6 | +5.3 | −3.5 | −2.6 | +7.9 |

Uncorrected, the banner shows 9 significant pairs. Holm keeps 8: all of the significant age differences, which is the structure the spec plants. It removes the one regional letter, West over Midwest on top-2-box (p = 0.046 → 1.000). The spec has no regional effect on intent. Without correction, that letter would have reached the client deck as "intent is strongest in the West". Consideration in the South (57.1%) against the Northeast (46.9%) just misses even uncorrected (p = 0.0500).

### Step 7: Brand image banner (multi-select), Holm vs Benjamini–Hochberg (`req/07_image_banner_holm.json`, `req/08_image_banner_bh.json`)

The image question is a `set_u8` multi-select. Each slot turns one option into a 0/1 column with `ATTR_SET_HAS`, then runs the same one-row banner crosstab and column tests on the aware Q4 base. That gives 7 options × (age + region) = 14 slots and 63 tests. The two files differ only in `"method": "holm"` vs `"method": "bh"`.

```json
{"label": "trustworthy__region", "cohort": {"filename": "brand_tracker.pulse", "data_dir": "."},
 "weight": {"field": "weight", "kind": "probability"},
 "filterers": [{"type": "FILTER_INCLUDE", "field": "wave", "values": ["W4"]},
               {"type": "FILTER_NULL", "field": "attributes", "values": ["is_not_null"]}],
 "attributes": [{"type": "ATTR_SET_HAS", "field": "attributes", "label": "has_trustworthy",
                 "params": {"label": "Trustworthy"}}],
 "crosstab": {"rows": [{"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "market"}],
              "columns": [{"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "region"}],
              "cell": {"type": "AGG_AVERAGE", "field": "has_trustworthy", "label": "trustworthy"},
              "margins": {"rows": true}},
 "overlays": [{"name": "column_tests", "kind": "OVERLAY_PAIRWISE_PROP_Z", "scope": "column",
               "params": {"p_source": "cell_value"}}]}
```

| Eco-friendly | 23.6% | 21.5% | 24.2% | 25.3% | 20.5% | 19.4% | 27.5% | 24.4% |
| For people like me | 17.7% | 28.9% DE | 14.5% | 8.7% | 21.2% | 18.9% | 16.2% | 16.4% |

Uncorrected, the table has 10 significant pairs. Holm keeps 5 and BH keeps 6. All 10 are real: each corresponds to a dependency planted in the spec.

The two methods behave differently here because the 63 tests include many true nulls:

- Holm controls the chance of *any* false positive. To do that it gives up the Midwest's genuine 14-point trust lead over the South (raw p = 0.003 → Holm 0.172).
- BH controls the *share* of false positives among the hits. It keeps the trust lead (adjusted p = 0.031).

For a client banner that will be scanned for "where to act", BH is the better default. For a single headline claim to leadership, use Holm.

### Step 10: Client hand-off: SPSS file, labelled Excel, and a read-back check

```sh
pulse export predict -i brand_tracker.pulse --format spss --sanitize-names --json
pulse export spss    -i brand_tracker.pulse -o deliver/brand_tracker_2025.sav --sanitize-names --json
PULSE_LABEL_TABLES_DIR=./labels \
pulse export excel   -i brand_tracker.pulse -o deliver/brand_tracker_2025.xlsx \
      --labels wave=wave_names:augment --labels region=region_names:augment --json
pulse import spss    -i deliver/brand_tracker_2025.sav -o deliver/roundtrip.pulse --json
pulse api process    -r req/12_roundtrip_check.json --json
```

- **Export predict and the export.** Without `--sanitize-names`, export predict refuses with `PULSE_SPSS_NAME_INVALID`: multi-select options become SPSS indicator variables named after the option, and "Good value" is not a legal SPSS name. With the flag, the export writes 6,000 cases. It reports two warnings:
  - `PULSE_SPSS_NAME_SANITIZED` lists the five renames, such as `Good value` → `Good_value`.
  - `PULSE_SPSS_SIDECAR_ABSENT` is expected, because this cohort never came from SPSS.
- **What the `.sav` contains.** Each field description becomes the SPSS variable label. Categoricals (wave, age, gender, region) are string variables holding their category text. `attributes` becomes eight 0/1 dichotomies plus a multiple-dichotomy set definition, so it re-imports as a multi-select. `weight` is carried as a variable, and the client applies it with `WEIGHT BY weight`.
- **The Excel copy.** It keeps the codes and adds `wave_label` ("2025 Q1"…) and `region_label` ("Northeast"…) columns from the label tables.
- **Read-back check.** `req/12_roundtrip_check.json` is the Step 2 request pointed at the re-imported `.sav` cohort. It returns exactly the Step 2 figures: Q4 weighted unaided awareness 28.8% and weighted consideration 51.8%. So the client's file reproduces the deck.

## Implications for the brand team

- **Do not claim an awareness win for 2025.** Weighted unaided awareness is flat at 29–32%. The apparent gain is Q4's young-skewed sample.
- **NPS is slightly negative (−3.2), not positive.** It is polarised by age: +28 among 18–34s and −29 among people aged 55 and over. Any NPS target should be set and tracked on the weighted figure, by age band.
- **Consideration is the real 2025 story:** +17.5 points among the aware, with the Q4 step confirmed after correction. The Eco-friendly image gain tracks it closely.
- **Fix Q4 fieldwork quotas.** Weighting recovered the census mix, but at 63% efficiency. Q4 has the precision of 926 interviews, which is why some genuine Q4 differences cannot be confirmed.
- **Agree a correction policy with the client.** This example uses Holm for headline claims and BH for banner screening. Without one, about 1 in 20 null comparisons will show up as a letter.
